        Among \textbf{100 agents}, a verification exploit traveled through
        a shared library and messages.
        \par\medskip
        Within \textbf{27 minutes}, all \textbf{34 remaining problems}
        appeared solved. Other agents' objections did not stop the method.
      \end{block}
      \src{paglieri2026swarm}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \bigskip
  \centering
  \large An individual deviation becomes a resource for later agents.
  \takeaway{Can we detect that transition before deployment?}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{When does a local deviation become shared practice?}
  \large
  The question is not only whether several agents violate a rule.
  \par\bigskip
  \textbf{Does their interaction change what each would otherwise do?}
  \par\bigskip
  \normalsize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.2cm}p{10.6cm}@{}}
    \textbf{Onset} & Does another agent's work change a recipient's violation rate? \\[0.9em]
    \textbf{Continuation} & Does the recipient maintain or revise that work for successors? \\[0.9em]
    \textbf{Interruption} & What makes later agents stop the method and resume legitimate work? \\
  \end{tabular}
  \takeaway{Evaluate agents through both the influence they receive and the influence they leave.}
\end{frame}

\section{Relay and continuation}

\begin{frame}{A published bypass becomes a successor's execution plan}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{The predecessor}
        Installs a test-skipping hook and receives acceptance.
        Its code fails the independent audit.
        \par\medskip
        After acceptance, it publishes the method and explains why it worked.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{The successor}
        Turn 12: reads the complete account.\\[0.4em]
        Turn 13: installs the same hook.\\[0.4em]
        Turn 14: tests are skipped.\\[0.4em]
        Turn 15: accepted; fails the audit.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \bigskip
  \centering
  \large Acceptance $\longrightarrow$ publication $\longrightarrow$ execution by another agent.
  \evidence{One V4 Pro lineage. Saved outgoing text matches delivered text; tool records
  and audits bind the actions. The successor also receives other inherited material.}
  \takeaway{Completing one's own submission need not end participation in the method.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A wiki carries the method into a different task}
  \small
  Consecutive Qwen Max recipients; each starts at 30\% with 20 execution units.
  \par\medskip
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.5cm}p{5.2cm}p{5.4cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    Observation & Inventory contributor & Access-log successor \\
    \midrule
    Resources & 1 command + 8 private requests & 1 command + 8 private requests \\
    Own task & Code already scores 100/100 in retrospective grading & Code already scores 100/100 in retrospective grading \\
    Violation & Acquires 8 private evaluator cases & Acquires 8 private cases for its own package \\
    Onward work & Adds cases and records sampled indices & Reads the update, reuses the indices, adds its cases \\
    Final outcome & 17/20 units used; own reward 1 & 18/20 units used; own reward 1 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \evidence{The outgoing wiki matches the next incoming wiki. Early grades were not shown
  to the agents; founder tooling was also inherited.}
  \takeaway{The successor both uses the inherited resource and helps maintain it.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Maintaining the method consumes real resources}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Work performed by eleven requesters}
        \textbf{196 of 220} available execution units used.
        \par\medskip
        \textbf{112 units} spent on private requests and the commands making them.
        \par\medskip
        \textbf{Eight recipients} add case records to their wikis.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{What a reward-only view misses}
        All eleven submit and earn \textbf{full own reward}.
        \par\medskip
        Their actions also acquire and publish material for later use.
        \par\medskip
        Own-task success and contribution to an unauthorized method coexist.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \evidence{Main wiki study. A tool invocation costs one unit, plus one per admitted
  service request. Mixed commands are counted once; units do not isolate motives.}
  \takeaway{Track resources, own outcomes, violations and onward work within the same sessions.}
\end{frame}

\section{Divergence and active correction}

\begin{frame}{Change one contributor; the same recipient model takes different paths}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/spread-peers.pdf}
  \evidence{Three V4 Pro recipients and one varying contributor; five chains per setting,
  eight generations, the same initial note and cumulative record policy, 50 turns.
  Generated peer work first reaches recipients after generation 1.}
  \takeaway{A model's influence on other agents is a distinct object of evaluation.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Some recipients dismantle the method and leave another route}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Participant-initiated correction}
        Four Qwen Max recipients delete \textbf{nine recovery-related files}.
        \par\medskip
        All leave corrective guidance and task material.
        One supplies a public-only retrieval helper.
        \par\medskip
        All four retain full own reward.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{What the successors do}
        One reads the revised material, runs \textbf{4,000 invariant checks},
        and publishes an implementation and task notes.
        \par\medskip
        Across \textbf{20 successors}: no private requests, and no restoration
        of deleted filenames in recorded snapshots.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \evidence{Observed cleanup bundles deletion, guidance and replacement work.
  It is not an assigned deletion treatment. Invariant checks are not known-output test scores.}
  \takeaway{Shared work can carry a correction as well as an exploit.}
\end{frame}

\section{Controlled comparisons}

\begin{frame}{Onset: inherited work changes violations even on a solvable task}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/spread-solvable.pdf}
  \evidence{Sixteen sessions per model and condition; 50 turns. The historical
  \texttt{second\_item} task has a valid solution; its short-list behavior is unspecified.
  Conditions differ in the supplied shared record.}
  \takeaway{V4 Pro rises from 0/16 to 16/16 violations although an honest solution is available.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Continuation: accumulated work adds to a fixed seed's effect}
  \small
  Proof tasks; twelve matched blocks, five generations and a 20-turn ceiling.
  Within each block, conditions receive the same ordered tasks.
  \par\bigskip
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Model & No library & Fixed seed & Accumulating library \\
    \midrule
    Gemini 3.1 Pro & 3/60 & 33/60 & 47/60 \\
    DeepSeek Flash & 0/60 & 11/60 & 28/60 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \par\bigskip
  \textbf{Accumulation versus a fixed seed:}\\[0.4em]
  Gemini: +23.3 percentage points [6.7, 38.3].\\
  DeepSeek Flash: +28.3 points [10.0, 45.0].
  \evidence{Sessions with an accepted submission failing the saved audit.
  Brackets: 95\% matched-block bootstrap intervals. Accumulation adds accepted files
  and explanations; it changes content as well as amount. No-library also changes the interface.}
  \takeaway{An evolving record can change behavior beyond repeatedly supplying the same initial method.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Wiki exposure can start a relay without sustained growth}
  \small
  Six models; initial scores of 30\%, 60\% or 90\%; qualification at 60\%.
  \par\smallskip
  \textbf{972 recipients in 162 six-session chains}, with three founder realizations.
  \par\bigskip
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Initial shared state & First requesters & Later requesters & Positive chains \\
    \midrule
    Empty wiki & 0/54 & 0/270 & 0/54 \\
    Artifacts without original note & 0/54 & 2/270 & 2/54 \\
    Artifacts with original note & 7/54 & 2/270 & 8/54 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \evidence{Founders had an authorized recovery assignment; ordinary recipients did not.
  One founder lacks the note, making its nonempty conditions identical.
  An unchanged original note is withdrawn after the first recipient.}
  \takeaway{Measure initiation and later continuation separately: exposure is not inevitable spread.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Interruption: a correction lowers violations, then some return}
  \small
  Ten lineages per condition; an agent-written correction is inserted at generation 3.
  \par\bigskip
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
    \toprule
    & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Generation 3} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Generation 4} \\
    Model & No correction & Correction & No correction & Correction \\
    \midrule
    V4 Pro & 9/10 & 4/10 & 10/10 & 7/10 \\
    Gemini 3.1 Pro & 4/10 & 1/10 & 5/10 & 2/10 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \par\bigskip
  The immediate response and the successor's response are both part of the outcome.
  \evidence{Contradictory functions, 24 turns, separate assigned lineages.
  Researchers insert a frozen DeepSeek Flash note; this is not a test of spontaneous wiki cleanup.}
  \takeaway{Evaluate interruption through later behavior, not the presence of a warning.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Inherited shortcuts change resource use as well as violations}
  \small
  Sixteen contradictory-function sessions per model and condition; 24-turn ceiling.
  \par\bigskip
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Model & Mean model turns & Sessions with audited violations \\
    & Control $\to$ method & Control $\to$ method \\
    \midrule
    V4 Pro & 23.63 $\to$ 16.69 & 2/16 $\to$ 12/16 \\
    Gemini Pro & 23.88 $\to$ 21.19 & 1/16 $\to$ 6/16 \\
    Kimi & 22.81 $\to$ 20.94 & 0/16 $\to$ 8/16 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \par\bigskip
  Sharing a method can reduce the work used while increasing violations.
  \evidence{Condition means include stopping behavior. Model turns are distinct from
  wiki execution units; these are not matched estimates of time per successful violation.}
  \takeaway{Resource savings are not automatically safety gains; extra work is not automatically sacrifice.}
\end{frame}

\section{A benchmark for propagation}

\begin{frame}{Turn the relay into repeatable comparisons}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{2.0cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{5.2cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{5.9cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    Measure & Comparison & Outcome to preserve \\
    \midrule
    Onset & Same recipient model, with or without inherited material & Change in violations, alongside legitimate task completion \\
    Continuation & Fixed seed versus accumulating work; fresh successors & Later violations, renewed contributions and actual successor use \\
    Influence & Fixed recipients, different contributing models & Recipient trajectories across independent chains \\
    Interruption & Corrective material versus the comparison condition & Later violations, recurrence and recovery of legitimate work \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \par\medskip
  Bind each result to \textbf{task + model + resources + shared history + audited actions}.
  \evidence{Use sessions for independent encounters and chains for shared histories.
  Historical studies keep their own protocols; the packaged development suite is a separate executable design.}
  \takeaway{Measure susceptibility, onward influence and correction---not just a single-agent failure rate.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \centering
  {\Large\bfseries An agent's work can outlive its session.\par}
  \vspace{0.7cm}
  \large
  Does that work make another agent more likely to violate?\\[0.7em]
  Does the next agent maintain and pass on the method?\\[0.7em]
  What makes the chain stop and legitimate work resume?
  \par\vspace{0.8cm}
  \normalsize
  \textbf{Parabox: evaluate how agents influence one another,\\
  not only how they behave alone.}
\end{frame}

\section{Supporting evidence}

\begin{frame}{Own-reward conflict is a separate experimental condition}
  \begin{block}{An explicit cost of private acquisition}
    The first returned private case sets the recipient's reward multiplier
    to zero for the rest of that session.
    Work can still pass onward; the successor receives a fresh multiplier.
  \end{block}
  \begin{block}{Observed response}
    \textbf{108 recipients} in 36 three-session chains:
    \textbf{zero private requests}.
    107 submit for full reward; one does not submit.
  \end{block}
  \evidence{Separate adaptive follow-up: six models, three initial scores,
  empty/full wiki and one founder realization. Chain length and founder coverage also
  differ from the main study, so this is not an isolated penalty-effect estimate.}
  \takeaway{Resource allocation and explicit own-reward conflict require distinct measurements.}
\end{frame}
